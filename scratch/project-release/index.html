AlphaNIST: learning assembly with binary task rewards

Hi everyone,

We’re sharing AlphaNIST, a study of learning contact-rich robotic assembly through large-scale reinforcement learning. We ask how much manipulation strategy a robot can learn when we specify successful assembly, rather than the steps for getting there.

In simulation, we train one policy from scratch across all twenty tasks on NIST Assembly Task Board #1, spanning nut threading, gear meshing, and peg, rod, and connector insertion. All tasks share a training configuration, without demonstrations, scripted manipulation stages, or rewards for intermediate assembly progress. Training combines diverse, physically valid starting states with adaptive reset-state sampling.

A few highlights:

  • Shared learning across assemblies. In the reported single-component comparison, the shared policy reaches 98.2% training success, versus 81.4% on average for twenty separately trained policies. These metrics cover mixed starting states, including loose parts and partial assemblies.
  • Dexterous behaviors without maneuver-specific supervision. Selected runs show the policy changing an unsuitable grasp, using the board to reorient a connector, and tapping a nut to advance its thread. These individual maneuvers are neither demonstrated nor directly rewarded.
  • Coordination across longer sequences. Separately trained multi-component policies complete ten-component assemblies, with the task-success reward issued only after every active component is assembled. No assembly order is prescribed. Selected runs show a policy moving obstacles aside or preparing one part, working on another, and returning to finish the first.

The results suggest that shared experience across different assemblies can help a policy acquire skills that are difficult to learn in isolation.
