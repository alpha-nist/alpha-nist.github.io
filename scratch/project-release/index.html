Internal project release: AlphaNIST

Project: AlphaNIST: Emergent assembly skills from binary task rewards

Team: Omitted from this public review copy; add privately before sending.

Keywords: Robotic assembly; Contact-rich manipulation; Multi-task reinforcement learning; Binary task rewards; Long-horizon control

Summary: Robotic assembly requires precise contact interactions and extended sequences of grasping, reorientation, and insertion. We present AlphaNIST, a reinforcement-learning framework that trains one policy from scratch across all twenty tasks on NIST Assembly Task Board #1 in simulation. Training combines diverse, physically valid starting states, adaptive reset-state sampling, and large-scale parallel experience under a binary task-success objective. All tasks share one training configuration, without demonstrations, scripted manipulation stages, assembly-progress rewards, or per-asset tuning.

Key results: In the reported single-component comparison, the shared policy reaches 98.2% training success, versus 81.4% on average for twenty separately trained policies. Selected runs show grasp adaptation, board-assisted connector reorientation, and fingertip tapping for nut threading, without directly supervising these maneuvers. Separately trained multi-component policies complete ten-component assemblies, with the task-success reward issued only when every active component is assembled and no prescribed assembly order. Selected runs show a policy preparing one component, assembling another, and returning to finish the first without an explicit planner.

Limitations: All results are in simulation; sim-to-real transfer has not been demonstrated. The reported success rates are training metrics across mixed starting states, including partial assemblies, rather than held-out performance from fully randomized starts. The videos show selected successful runs, not the frequency or reliability of each observed behavior. Continuous twenty-component assembly remains outside the demonstrated scope.
