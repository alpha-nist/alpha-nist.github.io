Robotic assembly · In simulation

Learning assembly without prescribed manipulation stages.

Assembly specifies an outcome, not a sequence of moves. AlphaNIST studies how grasping, reorientation, and insertion can be learned from task rewards, cached starting states, and parallel experience.

Single-component study

One policy, different assembly problems.

A shared policy trains across twenty NIST assembly tasks: gears, nuts, rods, pegs, and connectors. Its inputs are point clouds and robot state.

01 / Learning setup

A clear objective. Room to find a way.

The policy is not given a grasp–lift–insert program. Training supplies a distribution of physically valid starting states and rewards the completed assembly.

  1. 01

    Start from varied states

    A cached bank includes loose parts, in-hand grasps, and partial assemblies. These are starting states, not demonstrations of how to finish.

  2. 02

    Spend experience where it helps

    Beta sampling emphasizes states the policy sometimes solves, while retaining a chance to sample every state. Parallel environments provide more experience.

  3. 03

    Reward the finished task

    The task reward is binary, alongside per-step control costs. Reaching, grasping, and partial insertion receive no progress rewards in this condition.

02 / Single-component evidence

Success improves with parallel training scale.

Binary task rewards with beta sampling yield the highest evaluated success among these three conditions. The comparison isolates the tested sampling and reward choices—not every possible form of reward shaping.

Evaluation protocol and scope

Each evaluation seed contains 32 trials per task: 608 episodes, with equal weight per task. Resets are sampled uniformly within each task from the shared frozen bank. Success is the first full assembly without a simultaneous safety failure; unfinished episodes fail at 15 seconds.

All nine scales are measured; lines only connect observations. Environment count, GPU count, and rollout batch size grow together at a fixed iteration count, so this is not a compute-matched comparison. The chart reports means, without error bars.

03 / Qualitative extension

When several parts share the board.

With ten active components, placement changes what remains accessible. Separately trained K10 policies learn longer assembly sequences, without a prescribed assembly order.

K10 · Separately trained policies

These four selected reset-to-success runs are qualitative examples. They are not the K1 policy, a continuous rollout, or a numerical K10 success-rate evaluation.

The research question

How much dexterity and coordination can emerge when we specify the assembly goal, but leave the sequence of actions to learning?
