The learning problem

Specify the outcome.
Leave the maneuvers open.

Assembly involves more than bringing a part to a target. Contact changes a grasp; a tooth blocks a gear; another object occupies the approach. The policy must respond to what happens.

One shared single-component policy, K1, learns across twenty NIST assembly tasks. The multi-component policies are trained separately. The K10 policy shown above must assemble all ten active parts; neither their order nor the individual maneuvers are prescribed.

Observations
Point clouds and robot state provide the policy’s input.
Task reward
A binary signal for completed assembly. Reaching, grasping, and partial insertion have no task-progress rewards; control costs are retained.
Starting states
A cached bank of physically valid states: loose parts, in-hand grasps, and partial assemblies. These resets are not demonstrations.
Experience
Large-scale parallel training with beta sampling of starting states, emphasizing states the policy sometimes solves while keeping every state eligible.

What these observations establish

These are examples of learned assembly behavior in simulation, not a claim of reliability on physical hardware. Successful clips alone cannot establish how often a policy succeeds.

Keep the evaluation conditions in view

Success measurements depend on the task, policy, and reset distribution. In particular, mixed starting-state results can include held parts and partial assemblies; they should not be read as loose-part-only success.
