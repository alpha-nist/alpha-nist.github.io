01:03–01:22  Reward the outcome

We use a binary task reward for completed assembly. Reaching, grasping, and partial insertion receive no assembly-progress reward. Separate control costs discourage large actions, rapid motion, and excessive torque. The objective specifies the result, not a rewarded sequence of maneuvers.

On screen: Simple outcome diagram: reach, grasp and partial insertion receive no assembly-progress reward; complete assembly receives the task reward. Explicit small note for control costs.

01:22–01:44  Make useful experience available

Threading shows another kind of contact-driven motion. On the small nut, repeated one-sided contacts advance the thread. On the larger nut, the fingers move around it through successive contacts. We call these fingertip tapping and circular threading: different solutions, not instructions written into the task.

04:42–05:00  What this establishes—and what remains open

All results are in simulation. Physical transfer and continuous twenty-component assembly under one terminal objective remain open. What we see here is that diverse starts and shared experience can support several kinds of dexterous behavior under a common completion objective.
